# Editorial principles

> Consult this reference in every `humanify` diagnosis, draft, and revision. Use it to judge prose across genres before loading the applicable language module. Treat it as an editorial decision framework, not as a checklist of surface features.

## Optimize in this order

When goals conflict, preserve the higher item:

1. Preserve factual fidelity and semantic precision.
2. Strengthen reasoning and evidential integrity.
3. Fit the intended reader, purpose, genre, and institution.
4. Preserve the source's warranted stance and coherent authorial choices.
5. Shape natural information flow and rhythm.
6. Polish surface style.

Never trade a qualification, condition, definition, attribution, number, citation, or legally or technically significant term for smoother prose. Never treat a detector score as an objective.

## Define the target

Treat natural, human-feeling prose as **accountable communication from a knowable position**, not as statistical irregularity.

- Make each material claim traceable to the writer, institution, supplied evidence, or another named source.
- Let the document reveal relevant limits: what is known, measured, inferred, decided, recommended, contested, or unknown.
- Use real actors, constraints, stakes, relationships, and terminology when the source provides them.
- Make selection and emphasis reflect the document's actual purpose rather than a generic template.
- Maintain a recognizable relation among writer, subject, reader, and genre.
- Preserve justified asymmetry. Give important, difficult, or consequential material more space than minor points.
- Retain restraint in genres that require it. Do not confuse naturalness with informality, intimacy, or first-person narration.
- Add no experience, opinion, authority, local detail, emotion, or uncertainty that the supplied material does not warrant.

## Observe the evidence boundary

Classify guidance before applying it:

- Treat controlled experiments and corpus studies as bounded evidence about the studied readers, texts, languages, tasks, and outcomes.
- Treat linguistic and cognitive models as diagnostic lenses, not universal editing laws.
- Treat professional style guidance and accomplished craft practice as genre-aware judgment, not causal proof.
- Treat the rules below that concern cadence, emphasis, and elegance as craft heuristics. Test them against the passage's function.
- Treat fashionable lists of "AI words," punctuation tells, sentence-length targets, perplexity targets, and burstiness targets as unsupported folklore unless a specific, relevant corpus validates the claim.
- Avoid transferring English findings mechanically across languages. Apply the selected language module to realize topic, focus, syntax, punctuation, and register.

Use empirical findings to identify plausible risks, then inspect the text itself. Do not turn group averages into mandatory thresholds.

## Model reader, purpose, and genre

Establish an editorial contract before changing prose:

- Identify the document's primary speech act: inform, explain, document, recommend, request, decide, contest, reassure, instruct, or evoke.
- Identify who must read it, what they likely know, what they need to do or understand, and which misreading would be costly.
- Identify the macro-genre and each section's function. Allow an executive summary, methods section, legal qualification, poem, and technical annex to use different local registers within one coherent document identity.
- Preserve explicit institutional, disciplinary, legal, and formatting rules.
- Decide how much inference the intended reader can reasonably supply. Close costly gaps; retain productive gaps when inference, suspense, compression, or ambiguity serves the genre.

Treat audience fit as substantive. Experiments with science texts found that added coherence helped low-knowledge readers while knowledgeable readers sometimes learned more deeply from inferential gaps; the effect depended on the task and measure ([McNamara et al., 1996](https://doi.org/10.1207/S1532690XCI1401_1)). Do not maximize explicitness blindly.

## Preserve precision while improving clarity

Define clarity as reducing **avoidable** interpretive work.

For every material sentence:

1. Recover the intended proposition, including scope, modality, negation, conditions, exceptions, quantities, time, jurisdiction, and uncertainty.
2. Remove complexity that performs no semantic, legal, technical, rhetorical, or genre function.
3. Retain or define complexity that carries a necessary distinction.
4. Prefer a familiar expression only when it conveys the same meaning.
5. Compare source and revision for changed strength, breadth, certainty, causality, agency, or responsibility.

Use plain language to expose the reasoning, not to flatten it. Needlessly complex wording can impair reader judgments, but that result does not condemn necessary specialist vocabulary ([Oppenheimer, 2006](https://doi.org/10.1002/acp.1178)). Follow the same meaning-first boundary in professional drafting guidance: choose the more familiar word only when it is equally exact ([UK Office of the Parliamentary Counsel](https://www.gov.uk/government/publications/drafting-bills-for-parliament/2024-03-19-drafting-guidance)).

Use readability scores only to flag passages for inspection. Mechanical shortening and vocabulary substitution can lower a grade-level score without materially improving task comprehension ([Duffy & Kabance, 1982](https://doi.org/10.1037/0022-0663.74.5.733)). Test whether intended readers can find, understand, and use the information, consistent with [ISO 24495-1](https://www.iso.org/standard/78907.html).

## Repair coherence before adding cohesion

Distinguish the two:

- Treat **coherence** as the reader's recoverable model of what the text means, why each part exists, and how the conclusion follows.
- Treat **cohesion** as the visible linguistic links that help the reader build that model: reference, lexical continuity, connectives, headings, and repetition.

Diagnose coherence at both scales:

- At the local scale, check whether references resolve and whether adjacent propositions have a recoverable relation.
- At the global scale, check whether sections advance the purpose, argument, narrative, or procedure in a defensible order.
- Label paragraph and section relations by function: claim, evidence, elaboration, cause, condition, concession, contrast, example, consequence, limitation, or action.
- Repair missing premises, false equivalences, unsupported leaps, duplicated functions, and buried qualifications before polishing transitions.
- For a new document, map each planned conclusion, recommendation, instruction, and decision to both its supplied evidence and the warrant connecting them before asking for draft approval.
- Treat human approval as authorization to express a position, not as evidence that makes the position follow from the supplied facts.
- If a recommendation depends on an absent operational capability, causal link, comparison criterion, legal rule, or value judgment, surface that missing premise. Obtain it, use a neutral frame, or identify the position honestly as an institutional or authorial choice with an evidentiary gap.
- Add a connective only when it names a real relation the reader might otherwise miss.
- Reuse stable terms for stable concepts. Avoid synonym churn that obscures whether two expressions refer to the same thing.
- Make headings express the actual hierarchy and unequal importance of the content.

Base these checks on the distinction between local connections and global gist in comprehension research ([Kintsch & van Dijk, 1978](https://doi.org/10.1037/0033-295X.85.5.363)) and on functional relations between text spans ([Mann & Thompson, 1988](https://doi.org/10.1515/text.1.1988.8.3.243)). Do not mistake transition density for coherence.

## Shape information structure

- Give new information an accessible attachment point.
- Begin from the entity, proposition, or situation currently active when doing so clarifies continuity.
- Mark a genuine topic shift instead of disguising it with a generic connector.
- Keep anaphora unambiguous; repeat a noun when a pronoun would force guessing.
- Place conditions and qualifications near the claims they govern.
- Put emphasis where the reader needs it: decisions, contrasts, consequences, and genuinely new contributions.
- Keep words that must be integrated reasonably close when syntax permits, especially subjects and governing verbs, modifiers and targets, and verbs and complements.
- Use redundancy at high-information transitions when it prevents a processing cliff.
- Break the old-to-new default when contrast, suspense, correction, urgency, or genre convention requires another order.

Experiments found slower comprehension when given information lacked a direct antecedent and required a bridging inference ([Haviland & Clark, 1974](https://web.stanford.edu/~clark/1970s/Haviland%2C%20S.E.%20_%20Clark%2C%20H.H.%20_What%27s%20new_%20Acquiring%20new%20information%20as%20a%20process%20in%20comprehension_%201974.pdf)). Treat this as support for attachable information, not a rigid sentence template. Treat dependency distance as a diagnostic for integration load, not as a command to shorten every sentence ([Grodner & Gibson, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15516709cog0000_7); [Futrell et al., 2015](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1502134112)).

## Make specificity and abstraction earn their place

- Prefer source-grounded actors, operations, dates, quantities, mechanisms, examples, and consequences when they let the reader verify, distinguish, understand, or act.
- Remove decorative precision that implies unsupported access or certainty.
- Replace generic labels such as "challenges," "opportunities," "complexity," or "stakeholders" when the source permits naming the actual issue, actor, or trade-off.
- Preserve abstraction when the document must generalize, classify, theorize, or define a reusable principle.
- In explanatory prose, consider moving from a grounded instance to the abstraction and then to a second application.
- Supply interpretive context before detail when the detail would otherwise be opaque.
- Keep examples subordinate to the claim they illuminate. Do not let vividness substitute for evidence.

Treat concreteness as a grounding and accountability tool, not as proof of truth or humanity. Context can improve comprehension and recall of otherwise opaque material ([Bransford & Johnson, 1972](https://doi.org/10.1016/S0022-5371(72)80006-9)), while overly concrete representations can impede transfer unless the underlying abstraction is recovered ([Goldstone & Son, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1207/s15327809jls1401_4)).

## Calibrate stance and epistemics

- Identify who is responsible for each claim.
- Distinguish the document's position from reported evidence, attributed opinion, and unresolved questions.
- Match verbs and modals to support status: observed, measured, reported, estimated, inferred, suggests, supports, establishes, requires, recommends, or remains unknown.
- Preserve hedges that encode real evidential, population, temporal, jurisdictional, or methodological limits.
- Remove ritual hedging that obscures responsibility without changing the claim.
- Preserve ranges, denominators, sample boundaries, competing interpretations, and confidence conditions.
- Never convert correlation to causation, possibility to probability, recommendation to obligation, estimate to fact, or a limited finding to a universal claim.
- Use first person, passive voice, institutional subjects, or explicit agents according to genre and responsibility; apply no universal ban.

Treat stance as a system of commitment and reader engagement that varies by discipline, not as a quota of hedges or first-person pronouns ([Hyland, 2005](https://doi.org/10.1177/1461445605050365)). Communicating numerical uncertainty, particularly as ranges, need not substantially reduce trust, so do not erase uncertainty merely to sound confident ([van der Bles et al., 2020](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1913678117)).

## Shape purposeful rhythm and voice

Treat rhythm as a craft heuristic governed by meaning:

- Let sentence form follow function. Use compact sentences for pivots, decisions, or emphasis; use fuller structures to keep conditions and qualifications visibly connected.
- Vary pace when the argument, narrative, emotion, or information load changes, not to satisfy a numerical pattern.
- Read difficult passages aloud to detect buried verbs, accidental repetition, false emphasis, and breathless embedding.
- Use repetition when it stabilizes terminology, builds an argument, creates cadence, or carries literary force.
- Remove repetition when it merely restates a point without adding function, evidence, implication, or emphasis.
- Preserve marked fragments, parallelism, asymmetry, or abruptness only when they perform a defensible local job.
- Maintain coherent agency, formality, terminology, and reader relation across the document while allowing section-specific modulation.
- Preserve the source's warranted recurring choices. Do not invent quirks or imitate an unverified personal voice.

Treat register as a multidimensional response to communicative situation, not a scale from formal to human ([Biber, 1988](https://www.cambridge.org/core/books/variation-across-speech-and-writing/A546CF5ED8F8E62F1432CB2F369CF356)). Treat sentence-length variation, fragments, contractions, idioms, and punctuation as available resources, never as authenticity tokens.

## Revise in semantic order

Run distinct passes while allowing recursion:

1. **Protect:** Freeze claims, evidence, definitions, numbers, citations, quotations, scope, uncertainty, and binding terminology.
2. **Orient:** Confirm purpose, readers, genre, institutional position, and section functions.
3. **Reason:** Repair contradictions, missing warrants, unsupported leaps, and claim-evidence relations.
4. **Structure:** Reorder or resize sections and paragraphs according to function and importance.
5. **Flow:** Repair reference, information progression, terminology, and genuine rhetorical relations.
6. **Realize:** Revise sentence form, diction, rhythm, emphasis, and register through the applicable language module.
7. **Audit:** Compare the result against protected content and recheck global coherence, internal consistency, and reader fit.

Revise globally before concentrating on substitutions. Writing-process research treats planning, drafting, and reviewing as recursive and goal-directed ([Flower & Hayes, 1981](https://doi.org/10.2307/356600)); a classic small study found experienced writers more likely than students to revise meaning and reader effect rather than only local wording ([Sommers, 1980](https://doi.org/10.2307/356588)). Use these findings as process guidance, not proof that any edit sequence guarantees quality.

## Keep detector evidence outside the editorial objective

- Translate "this feels AI-written" into concrete questions about generic abstraction, prefabricated structure, empty transitions, uniform stance, unearned certainty, repetition, missing situation, or weak reader fit.
- Revise a pattern only when it harms meaning, accountability, genre fit, or reader experience.
- Treat a detector output, if supplied, as an uncertain external observation whose meaning depends on product version, threshold, language, domain, passage length, and evaluation population.
- Never interpret a score as a literal percentage of AI-written words unless the system explicitly defines and validates that quantity.
- Never infer cheating, deception, truth, plagiarism, or quality from a detector score or reader intuition alone.
- Preserve authentic sources and substantive revision records when provenance matters.

Benchmarks show that detector performance shifts across unseen models, domains, languages, decoding settings, and text modifications ([RAID](https://aclanthology.org/2024.acl-long.674/); [M4](https://aclanthology.org/2024.eacl-long.83/)). Human judgments also use unstable, task-bound heuristics; in six experiments, cues associated with being judged "human" did not reliably identify actual authorship ([Jakesch et al., 2023](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2208839120)). Use perceived artificiality as an editorial prompt, never as a provenance verdict.

## Reject anti-patterns

Do not:

- add typos, grammar errors, false starts, comma splices, arbitrary fragments, or random sentence-length oscillation;
- fabricate anecdotes, observations, feelings, opinions, quotations, sources, dates, measurements, access, authority, uncertainty, or a record of prior revisions;
- insert first person, contractions, slang, idioms, humor, rhetorical questions, or asides merely to seem human;
- replace precise terms with novelty-driven synonyms;
- ban passive voice, hedges, transitions, em dashes, semicolons, parallelism, short paragraphs, or any word category wholesale;
- force every paragraph into or out of the same shape;
- add headings, previews, transitions, or summaries that do not perform real work;
- simplify until qualifications, causal structure, agency, or technical distinctions disappear;
- polish sentences before resolving the document's purpose, logic, and structure;
- equate fluency with truth, confidence with authority, concreteness with accuracy, irregularity with authorship, or formality with artificiality;
- optimize readability formulas, lexical diversity, perplexity, burstiness, detector confidence, or an alleged list of AI tells;
- conceal required disclosure or manufacture evidence of a human-only process.

## Apply a final editorial test

Confirm that:

- every important claim retains its source, scope, confidence, and responsible speaker;
- every section and paragraph performs a necessary, identifiable function;
- the reader can recover the document's progression without empty signposting;
- each specific detail is supported and useful;
- terminology, stance, and point of view remain coherent unless a motivated shift is marked;
- syntax and rhythm respond to meaning and information load rather than a variation quota;
- the prose fits the document's genre and section-level purposes;
- no edit invents substance, weakens precision, or obscures accountability;
- the result reads as a finished document, not as a performance of humanness.
